# Goals and habits: from plans to steady progress

**Covers:** SMART goal, SMART, OKR, Habit loop

## A goal is a direction, a habit is the engine {#overview}
Most people can set a goal. Far fewer reach it, usually for one of two reasons: the goal was too vague to act on, or the daily behavior that would move it never became routine. This article gives you two ways to write goals that are easy to check and one model for building the habits that carry them out.

## SMART goal {#smart-goal}
A SMART goal is a goal that passes five tests. Each letter stands for one of them.

- **Specific:** it says exactly what will be done.
- **Measurable:** you can tell how far along you are.
- **Achievable:** it is difficult but possible with your resources.
- **Relevant:** it matters for something you care about.
- **Time-bound:** it has a date.

"Get better at presenting" fails most tests. "Give three team presentations by 30 June and score at least 4 out of 5 in the audience survey" passes them all.

## SMART {#smart}
In meetings you will often hear just "make it SMART". The speaker is asking you to run the five checks above on your goal. A quick way to use it is to read your goal aloud and ask: what number, what date, who does what?

## OKR {#okr}
OKR stands for objectives and key results. An objective is a short, inspiring statement of what you want to achieve. Under it you write two to five key results, which are numbers that show whether you got there. Intel's Andy Grove developed the method, and John Doerr brought it to Google and made it popular.

OKRs are normally ambitious. A team often aims for results it has only a partial chance of reaching, and scoring 70 percent of the key results can count as a success. They are reviewed every quarter, and everyone can see the OKRs of other teams.

## Habit loop {#habit-loop}
The habit loop is the three-part cycle behind any habit: a cue triggers a routine, and the routine earns a reward. The journalist Charles Duhigg described it in his 2012 book, drawing on research done at MIT.

For example, the cue is the 9 a.m. alarm, the routine is a ten-minute review of the day's tasks, and the reward is the satisfaction of a clear plan. Repeating the loop makes the routine automatic.

To build a habit, pick a clear cue, make the routine small, and give yourself a real reward. To break one, keep the cue and the reward but swap the routine.

## A worked example {#example}
Amira wants to improve her writing at work.

The vague goal is "Write better emails". Make it SMART, one letter at a time:

| Letter | Amira's version |
|---|---|
| Specific | Put the request in the first line of every email |
| Measurable | Check ten emails a week against that rule |
| Achievable | It needs about two minutes per email |
| Relevant | She wants quicker replies from clients |
| Time-bound | By the end of the quarter |

As an OKR: *Objective:* become known as a clear writer. *Key results:* (1) 90 percent of sent emails open with the request, (2) average client reply time falls from 24 hours to 12, (3) three colleagues rate her writing 4 out of 5 or better.

Habit loop: cue is opening the "new email" window; routine is writing the request first; reward is ticking a box on a weekly chart.

If she hits 90 percent, a reply time of 15 hours and two ratings of 4, she has met one key result fully and two partly. On a simple average that scores about 80 percent, which is a strong quarter for an ambitious OKR.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Setting a goal without any plan for the daily routine. A goal states where you want to end up. The habit loop tells you what you do on Tuesday at 9 a.m. A second mistake is writing key results that describe tasks, such as "hold three meetings", not results, such as "customers renew at a higher rate".

## Key points {#key-points}
- A SMART goal is specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound.
- An OKR pairs an ambitious objective with measurable key results, reviewed regularly.
- A habit has a cue, a routine and a reward. Make the routine small.
- Pair every goal with a habit that makes progress daily.
