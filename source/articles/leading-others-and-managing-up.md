# Delegating, coaching and managing up

**Covers:** Delegation, Micromanagement, GROW model, Managing up

## Working through people {#overview}
As your career grows, more of your results come from other people: those you help, those who help you and the manager who sets your direction. Three skills cover the downward and upward relationships. You can give work away well, you can coach instead of command, and you can help your own manager succeed.

## Delegation {#delegation}
Delegation is giving someone else the responsibility and the authority to do a task, while you stay accountable for the result. Authority matters: if you hand over the task but not the right to make decisions, you have only passed on work.

Good delegation has a pattern. Choose the right person, explain the goal and why it matters, agree on what a good result looks like, set the deadline and check-in points, and give them the resources and the freedom to choose how. Afterwards, thank them and talk about what went well.

Tasks to delegate are those that someone else can do well enough, that help them grow, or that waste your time. Keep tasks that need your unique skills, confidential matters and your core decisions.

## Micromanagement {#micromanagement}
Micromanagement means controlling the small details of how someone does their work. It often comes from worry about quality, but it has costs: people lose ownership, wait for approval, hide problems and leave.

Signs include asking for constant updates, redoing other people's work, and giving step-by-step instructions to experienced staff. The cure is to focus on outcomes: agree on the goal and the check points, then step back. If a person struggles, increase support and review, but explain why, and reduce it again as they improve.

## GROW model {#grow-model}
The GROW model is a coaching conversation in four steps. Sir John Whitmore and others developed it in the 1980s.

1. **Goal:** What do you want to achieve?
2. **Reality:** Where are you now? What have you tried?
3. **Options:** What could you do? What else?
4. **Will:** What will you do, and by when? What might get in the way?

A coach mostly asks questions. The person finds their own answers, which makes them more likely to act on them.

## Managing up {#managing-up}
Managing up means building a good working relationship with your manager by understanding their goals and giving them what they need. It does not mean flattery. It is about making the relationship work for both of you.

Practical steps: learn what your manager's priorities and pressures are, find out how they like to receive updates (short email or a weekly talk), tell them about problems early with a proposed solution, and be clear about what you need from them, such as a decision or resources. Ask for feedback regularly.

## A worked example {#example}
Priya manages a team and wants Leo to take over the monthly report.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Delegation | She explains the goal (the director needs it on the 5th), gives Leo authority to change the layout, and agrees check-ins on the 2nd and 4th. |
| Avoiding micromanagement | She does not ask for daily updates. She only reviews the draft on the 2nd. |
| Coaching with GROW | When Leo is stuck on the data, she asks: "What would a good report look like? (Goal) What have you tried? (Reality) What else could you do? (Options) What will you do first? (Will)" |
| Managing up | Priya tells her own manager: "Leo now owns the report. I will give you a short update on the 5th, and I need you to approve the new format by Friday." |

The result is a task off Priya's desk, a growing colleague and a manager who knows what to expect.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Delegating the task but then taking it back at the first mistake. Leo learns not to take risks. A second mistake is delegating only the dull work: people can tell, and it destroys motivation. Give some stretching tasks as well.

## Key points {#key-points}
- Delegation hands over both the task and the authority, but you stay accountable.
- Micromanagement controls small details and reduces trust. Focus on outcomes.
- The GROW model guides a coaching talk through goal, reality, options and will.
- Managing up means understanding your manager's goals and giving them what they need.
